# S17: the SPU's voices played by sceSasCore, and an exact SPU that costs half

S9 step 3: the SPU's mixing moves to sceSasCore, the PSP's own voice mixer, which the PSP runs on
the Media Engine. `core/spu.c` stays the exact path and the fallback. The approach starts from
PsyZ `psp_sas.c`.

## 1. What moves, what stays

The core offers each voice at its key on, and once more when a sustained voice loops back
(`adopt`). The host (`sas.c`) takes the voice when the core can describe it as a VAG would hold
it (`SpuHwVoice`). For a taken voice:

- **sceSasCore does:** decoding, interpolation and mixing.
- **The SPU keeps:** everything the guest can read. Block walk, loop and IRQ addresses, ENDX,
  envelope and volume sweeps all stay in the SPU. The SPU walks the voice without decoding it
  (`shadow`), so ENDX and the IRQ fire at the same sample as before.

A voice goes back to the SPU when one of these happens:

- the guest turns on pitch modulation, noise or reverb for it;
- the guest moves its loop;
- the guest rewrites its flag bytes.

The VAG player reads a mirror of SPU RAM. In the mirror the flag bytes are rewritten
(`spu_hw_flag`), because the player stops at 7, loops at 3 and takes 6 as the loop start. Pages
the guest writes are copied when the write happens and applied at the same sample of the stream.
A voice reading a buffer that the guest refills therefore hears what the SPU would have.

`psxrt.spu` beside the EBOOT selects the mode:

| Value | Mode | What plays |
|---|---|---|
| `cpu` | 0 | the SPU mixes every voice (S13's path) |
| `both` | 2 | sceSasCore mixes the taken voices into a scratch buffer that is not heard, and the SPU mixes them too; the log compares the two levels |
| anything else, or no file | 1 (default) | sceSasCore plays the taken voices, mixed into the stream with `__sceSasCoreWithMix` |

## 2. What was wrong, and is fixed

- **The level was half.** A voice taken mid-play keeps its SPU envelope inside the volume, so
  the mixer's own envelope must stay at full level. The first ADSR for that was 0x000F/0x1FC0:
  sustain level at the top and decay rate 0. The decay still takes one step towards a sustain
  level at the top, and at rate 0 that step halves the level. The SPU's decay behaves the same
  way at rate 0. The voice then sat at 0x20000000 of 0x40000000. With decay rate 15
  (0x00FF/0x1FC0) one step takes 1/65536 off.
- **`shadow()` divided by zero.** When an envelope step was due on the current sample,
  `env_wait - 1` was -1. Cast to unsigned, it reached a division by a zero pitch step. The clamp
  now applies only to a positive count.
- **Voices the SPU must keep.** Pitch modulation (as source or target), noise, reverb, and loops
  the player cannot follow are refused at the offer. They are counted in the log
  (`refused pmon/noise/reverb/loop`).

## 3. Checked

**The SPU against S13, bit for bit.** A driver (`D:\Temp\PSX2PSP-Reborn\S9sas\difftest`) feeds
the S13 `spu.c` and the new one the same seeded random stream: register writes, key ons and
offs, reverb, IRQ addresses, XA input, and time steps of 0 to 220k samples. It compares
everything the guest can observe: the output stream, IRQs, and at every step ENDX, STAT, each
voice's walk and envelope state, and SPU RAM. Results:

- Mode 0 equals S13 on every seed.
- Mode 2 equals mode 0: the comparison mix changes nothing heard.
- In mode 1, the voices' walk equals mode 0 field by field. The `shadow` walk is exact.

**sceSasCore against the SPU, in PPSSPP.** A build that captured both streams in mode 2 (the SPU's
mix of the taken voices after the main volume, and sceSasCore's grains) from level 1 gave these
numbers:

| Measure | Before the fix | After the fix |
|---|---|---|
| Gain, sceSasCore over SPU | 0.5017 | 1.0034 |
| Correlation | 0.9995 | 0.9996 |
| Gain per 0.1 s | | 1.00 to 1.01 throughout |
| Lag | 0 samples (the grains land where the SPU's samples do) | |

The residual is -30 dB: 14.7 RMS on a 489 RMS signal. It is the SPU's Gaussian interpolation
against the PSP mixer's two-tap linear one. That one has slightly more treble, and the
first-difference energy is 1.09x. The PSP's mixer interpolates this way in hardware, so this is
the floor.

The per-second `rms spu/sas` of the log agrees within 1-2% with one voice. In a second where a
voice goes back to the SPU the gap is up to 8%, because the host applies the hand-back at the
nearest grain.

## 4. Cost (PPSSPP, relative only)

Level 1 from the snapshot, 10 s, PAL60, `cpu_shift` 1, on S16. The "now" column comes from a
run with no other PPSSPP open. Per §3 of S14, PPSSPP is no measure of real-PSP headroom.

| `psxrt.spu` | spu ms/s, first version | spu ms/s, now | audio thread in sceSasCore |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cpu` | 81 | 45 | 0 |
| `sas` (default) | 70 | 25 | 47 ms/s |
| `both` | | 51 | 49 ms/s |

For scale, the real PSP's log of the S15 build (S13's SPU, no sceSasCore) shows the SPU at about
110 ms/s in the intro.

The CPU path roughly halves, and all of the gain is exact (§3). The changes:

- **A sustain envelope at a fixed point stays put** (`env_still`). Its envelope is not stepped
  sample by sample.
- **Lap skipping.** A looping voice the SPU only walks skips whole laps of its loop (`skip`).
- **Silent reverb.** The reverb work area is skipped while it is at most 1 KB, all zero, and
  writes are off (`rev_silent`).
- **The final loop keeps its state in locals.** Its byte stores to SPU RAM could alias anything,
  so without this every field of `Spu` was reloaded each sample.
- **CD audio is read once per chunk** (`cd_audio` now takes n samples).

The audio thread's time inside sceSasCore (`sas thread`) is waiting. On a real PSP that work
runs on the Media Engine, not on the Allegrex. This is still to be confirmed from a real PSP's
`psxrt.log`.

## 5. Known limits

- **Capture.** The capture buffers of voices 1 and 3 hold silence while the host plays them. A
  game that reads its own voice output back would hear nothing. None does in level 1.
- **Hand-back.** A voice going back to the SPU in mode 1 restarts its ADPCM history at zero.
  This gives one block of slightly different samples. It can also change a later pitch
  modulation that reads this voice.
- **Real PSP.** Not yet run on hardware with this build. Expect `-- sas` lines with
  `errors 0` and `grains` about 172 a second while a voice plays, and `sas thread` in the
  bench line.
