# S18: the CPU stops waiting for the screen blit

## 1. What the S16b PSP log said

The S16b build ran on the real PSP (PAL60, `cpu_shift` 0). Its boot lines measured the device:

| | PSP | PPSSPP |
|---|---|---|
| 64 KB of vertices, uncached | 326 us | 213 us |
| the same, cached and written back | 700 us | 214 us |
| one `sceKernelGetSystemTimeLow` | 570 ns | 511 ns |
| one data cache miss | 216 ns | 29 ns |

The 1 KB stall step of S16 worked. The GE wait at the start of `present()` went from about
180 ms/s to 5-20 ms/s. Speed went up:

- intro: from 52% to 60-76%;
- level 1: from 54-58% to 50-73% (19-27 fps).

What was left in `present()` was its own part:

| | present | wait | draw | text |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| intro (MDEC) | 270-356 | 13-22 | 237-331 | 7-9 |
| menus | ~280 | 0-1 | 254-267 | 12 |
| level 1 | 146-201 | 3-9 | 131-188 | 6-8 |

All figures are ms per second.

The draw part is:

- a clear of the 480x272 screen;
- one scaled 480x272 sprite of the PS1 picture, from PS1 VRAM (a stride of 1024 pixels), bilinear;
- a `ge_sync()` that waits for both.

That is 4-5 ms a frame on the real GE, which PPSSPP draws in no time.

## 2. The blit in 32-pixel columns

One sprite as wide as the screen reads whole texture lines, wider than the GE's texture cache.
`draw_ps1` now draws 15 sprites, each 32 screen pixels wide. Each reads its own range of the
texture, with float texture coordinates so the columns meet exactly. The clear is drawn only when
the display is off, because the picture covers the whole screen.

## 3. The picture of frame N shown at frame N+1

`present()` no longer waits for its own list:

1. It writes the blit.
2. It lets the GE run it (`ge_run`: the stall moves to the end of the list, and the list stays open).
3. It goes back to the game.

The next frame's renderer commands go into the same list, after the blit. The next `present()`
starts with its `ge_sync()`, which waits for that frame's drawing and the blit before it. Then it:

1. prints the status line over the finished picture;
2. swaps the buffers;
3. draws the new picture into the other buffer.

The picture is one frame later on the screen. In exchange, the CPU no longer waits for the blit:
it was 4-5 ms of every frame.

Some cases still wait, as before:

- the software renderer (the GE reads PS1 VRAM in RAM, which the CPU is about to change);
- the stop screen;
- the two frames whose 24-bit conversion is checked against the CPU's.

The log's `present N (wait W draw D text T)` changes meaning:

- D is now the CPU writing the blit;
- the GE drawing it is in the next W.

## 4. The vertex ring uncached

The device wrote 64 KB of vertices in 326 us uncached and 700 us cached with the writeback.
`ring_ab` is now 0, so the ring stays uncached. The cached mode and the A/B switch are still in
the code, set by `ring_ab`.

## 5. Timings without the clock's own cost

A sampled interval (1 in 16) used to include one clock read: about 570 ns on the PSP, 16 times
over. `prof_init()` measures an empty timed interval at boot, and every sampled time subtracts it
(`prof_bias`). The log's ms are clamped at 0 (`ms_of`).

## 6. Checked in PPSSPP

- **Compare mode** from `level1.snap`, 14 s: 45-61k pixels a second differ from the software
  rasterizer, as in S16 (mostly 1-step dither and colour rounding). The blit does not touch PS1
  VRAM. A second whose transfer is half way now says so on its `-- ge:` line, instead of saying
  nothing.
- **Level 1 bench:** 100%, 59 vbl, 58.9 fps.
- **Intro bench:** 100%, 14.0 fps. On frames 60 and 600 the GE's 24-bit picture matches the
  CPU's, 0 pixels differ.

The real-PSP figures come from the next device run.

## 7. Figures for the cost of a polygon (S18b)

On the PSP, a polygon through the fast lane costs about 7 us in S15 and about 10 us in S16b.
PPSSPP charges about 2.5 us. The ring mode is not the cause: in S16b the cached and uncached
seconds cost the same. What changed from S15 to S16b is that the GE now draws while the CPU
walks the list. The next device log has the parts:

- **`kick N (M ms)`** on the `-- ge:` line: the stall moves (one system call every 1 KB of list),
  every one timed, less one clock read each.
- **`fast N (S setups M ms, C cached)`**: `fl_setup` (the GE state for a new key), one call in 16
  timed.
- **Boot line `ge busy`**: the GE draws 32 full-screen sprites textured from a page slot in RAM.
  Meanwhile the CPU times its cache misses on PS1 RAM and 64 KB of uncached vertex stores. These
  are compared with the idle figures of the line before. Then 256 stall moves are timed on their
  own. PPSSPP draws the sprites at once, so there the line says "the GE finished first".

The rest of the polygon cost is the `-- gpu: poly` time minus setup and kick: the vertices and
the lane's own work.
