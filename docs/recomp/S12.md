# S12: PS1 frames locked to the PSP display

After S11, level 1 at PAL60 (PPSSPP, `cpu_shift` 1) ran at 58.6 game frames a second while the
host slept 213 ms of every second. The CPU was not the limit; the clock was. PS1 time ran at the
emulated video rate, PAL60's 263 lines of 2168 cycles: 59.40 Hz against the PSP's 59.94 Hz. The
PS1 frames drifted against the PSP's vblanks, and some PSP frames showed a frame twice.

## 1. One PS1 frame per PSP frame

PS1 time now runs at one PS1 frame per PSP display frame (1001/60 ms). That is 0.2% fast for NTSC
and 0.9% for PAL60. `us_cycles` converts host time to PS1 cycles, and `sync_time` sleeps by the
inverse conversion.

At each PS1 vblank, after its frame is presented, `native_vsync` places the next PS1 frame on the
PSP's vblanks:

- **On time** (the PSP vblank count has not moved since the last PS1 vblank): the next frame
  starts at its slot, which ends `VS_MARGIN` (6 ms) before the next vblank but one. If the slot is
  still ahead, the host sleeps until it starts. Its swap (NEXTFRAME) then lands on its own vblank,
  even when its work varies by up to 6 ms.
- **Late**: the frame shows one vblank late. The next frame starts at the slot of the vblank that
  passed, so it runs without sleeping until it has caught up. PS1 time is never lost.

The vblanks' times come from a grid: one vblank is taken at boot, then a frame is 16683.3 µs. At
each call the grid is kept inside what the count says: vblank v has come, v + 1 has not. Drift
between the system timer and the display therefore corrects itself.

`sceDisplayWaitVblankStart` is not used. The first version waited on it, and PPSSPP's version,
called close to a vblank, waits for the next one: 4-6 waits a second took a whole frame (16.78 ms),
and the count moved by 2. Each such wait lost a PS1 frame: 56.1 fps.

## 2. Audio

At this rate the SPU produces 0.2-0.9% more samples than the PSP's 44.1 kHz output plays. The
audio thread now resamples by linear interpolation. Its step follows the ring's fill, smoothed
over 16 blocks, around a target of 3 blocks, within ±2%. Past 8 blocks of fill the excess is
trimmed. At 0.9% the pitch shift cannot be heard.

## 3. Measured

Bench, 10 s from the same level snapshot (frame 7000, PAL60, `cpu_shift` 1):

| | S11 | Wait on vblank | Grid, no margin | Grid + 6 ms margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| speed | 99% | 95% | 100% | 100% |
| vblanks a second | 58.6 | 56 | 59.9 | 59.9 |
| game fps (flips) | 58.6 | 56.1 | 59.0 | 58.7 |
| frames late a second | - | 43-50 | 38-48 | 0 |
| audio gaps a second | 0-11 | 0-11 | 0 | 0 |
| sleep ms/s | 213 | 242 | 207 | 204 |

The per-second log counts PSP vblanks with 59-60 PS1 frames in each. In the margin run, no frame
was late in any second and no vblank was skipped. Game flips are 0.3-1 a second below the vblanks;
the game drops these itself in its heaviest moments.

The busiest seconds still sleep only 40-70 ms. The headroom left is in the worst case, not the
average: the next costs are DMA2 and the GE (163 and 151 ms/s), then SPU mixing (75 ms/s).

The per-second log line adds `vsync wait N (ms) late N (avg, max us) skipped N`.
